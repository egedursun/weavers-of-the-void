# 15. DLC ideas

Status: **idea, not a plan.** This document splits the whole of spec 13 (depth expansion) into 85 themed expansions plus one free base update, as if each were sold separately, and gives each one back-of-the-box copy. The split is non-redundant: every mechanic of spec 13 belongs to exactly one expansion. The **Covers** line under each expansion names the spec 13 sections it holds. Integration still follows the waves of spec 13 Part J; this split does not change them.

---

## The Loom (free base update)

*Every thread is connected. Now you can see them all.*

Before the gods walk and the armies march, the world needs a loom. The Loom rebuilds Aerona's heart so that every cause has an effect and every effect has a reason you can read.

- **The Why of Everything**: hover over any number in the world and see exactly what made it so.
- **Chains of Consequence**: every battle, death and betrayal is recorded with its cause, so stories trace themselves across centuries.
- **Nothing Lost, Nothing Made from Air**: people, goods, coin and souls are counted to the last one.
- **Your Hand on Every Lever**: run any mechanic now, suppress it, edit it or pin it in place, all of it undoable.
- **The Harshness Dial**: heroic, realistic or brutal. One dial sets how deadly the world is.
- **Faster than Ever**: a multicore engine that turns more of the world in less time.

**Covers:** F9, F10, F28, I.1, J.7, J.11, J.16, J.16c, J.19, G11, W0.

## I. World and Nature

### 1. Turning Seasons

*Fifty years pass in an epoch. Now you will feel every winter.*

The year has a rhythm at last. Harvests fail in one cruel summer, rivers freeze in one long winter, and every storm is drawn from the real sky above a real region.

- **Seasons of Your Making**: two to six seasons per world, shaped by tilt, moons and world type.
- **Weather That Matters**: fog that blinds archers, blizzards that bury armies, storms that sink fleets.
- **The Year of Ash Rain**: the worst year of every epoch is remembered by name in chronicles, legends and fasts.
- **Calendars and Eras**: every people counts time its own way, from regnal years to the god's descent.

**Covers:** F1, F2.2, F2.3, F1 RP3.

### 2. The Living Land

*The map was a picture. Now it is a place.*

Every region now has soil, rainfall, forests, rivers and wounds. Clear the forests and the rains fail. Abandon the fields and the wild returns.

- **Soil, Sun and Rain**: twenty-two terrain kinds and ten soils, each read by harvests, armies and settlers.
- **Scars on the Earth**: battlefields, salted fields and plague grounds that last for generations.
- **Ley Lines**: rivers of magic across the land, with nexuses worth killing for.
- **Blessed, Cursed and Savage Lands**: regions where the dead rise, where springs heal, where beasts grow huge.
- **Danger with Teeth**: bandits, beasts and avalanches that actually harm travellers and settlers.

**Covers:** F2.1, F2.4–F2.6, 6.1 (base, alignment axes, succession), 6.2 (base).

### 3. Stone and Deep

*Dig too deep, and something digs back.*

Beneath every region lies a column of stone, water and darkness. Miners follow veins through limestone and granite into caverns no surface eye has seen.

- **Layered Geology**: real rock layers with veins of ore, marble for temples and aquifers for wells.
- **The Underworld Beckons**: three cavern layers, a magma sea and a deep realm beneath them all.
- **Rivers That Live**: flow, floods, salinity and dams that let one realm starve another.
- **Quakes and Fire Mountains**: tectonics that raise new land and bury old cities.
- **Seal the Deep**: gates, rites and collapsed tunnels to hold back what you woke.
- **Bones of Ages Past**: fossil beds that feed dragon legends and scholarly heresies.

**Covers:** 6.1 RP (geology, strata, hydrology, tectonics), 6.1 RP8 (sealing), 6.3 RP (cave-ins), 6.2 RP7 (fossils).

### 4. Winds of the World

*Deserts where mountains steal the rain. Famine where the monsoon fails.*

The air moves at last. Winds blow by the world's own spin and sun, oceans carry warmth and fish, and great climate swings bring droughts to lands a world apart.

- **Prevailing Winds**: trade-wind bands that set sailing routes and spread fire.
- **Rain Shadows**: deserts and forests where physics, not chance, puts them.
- **Monsoons**: seasons of rain that feed millions, until the year they don't come.
- **Ocean Currents**: warm coasts, rich fisheries and castaways carried across the sea.
- **The Breathing Planet**: carbon, water and nutrient cycles that answer every forest burned.

**Covers:** 10.7, 10.7 RP5, 10.9 RP3.

### 5. Plough and Net

*Feed the many, or watch them march on your granaries.*

Agriculture becomes a living economy of rings, rotations and rights. Hunger drives invention, irrigation salts the soil, and the commons become a battlefield.

- **Rings of Land**: gardens near the town, grain beyond, herds at the edge, and roads that redraw it all.
- **Market Towns Arise**: settlements spaced as trade demands.
- **Hunger Breeds Invention**: population pressure pushes terraces, irrigation and crop rotation.
- **The Soil Remembers**: nutrient stocks that drain, recover or turn to salt over centuries.
- **Commons and Enclosure**: shared pastures, the lords who fence them and the families they displace.
- **Nets and Herds**: fish stocks that boom and collapse, closed seasons, milking, shearing and draught beasts.

**Covers:** 4.2 RP4 (rings, central places), 6.1 RP4, 6.1 RP5, 6.1 RP8 (closures), 3.2 RP (husbandry).

### 6. Wild Kingdoms

*Every creature has a range, a hunger and a place in the chain.*

Species leave the spreadsheet and enter the land. Herds migrate with the seasons, wolves follow the deer, and the people who tame the wild become something new.

- **Living Ranges**: populations per region that grow, migrate and vanish.
- **The Food Web**: when the prey is gone, the predators come for your livestock.
- **From Wild to Breed**: tame, herd, domesticate and breed new kinds of animals.
- **Flora**: crops, herbs, dyes and poisons as living species with seasons and needs.
- **Vermin and Companions**: rats in the granary, cats to hunt them, hounds at the hearth.

**Covers:** 3.2 (base), 3.2 RP (flora, vermin), 3.3a RP (pets).

### 7. Tooth and Claw

*Nature does not stand still. It adapts, or it dies.*

Evolution runs in your world. Hunted beasts grow wary, plagues grow fierce in crowded cities, and after every extinction, life rushes in to fill the gap.

- **Selection Pressure**: traits shift with predators, climate, hunters and even the gods' protection.
- **Niches and Rivals**: two species cannot share one niche for long.
- **Radiation After Ruin**: survivors of mass extinction become the ancestors of new worlds.
- **Partners and Parasites**: pollinators, ticks, mimics and the soil's unseen workers.
- **Senses Beyond Ours**: night sight, echolocation, scent and spirit sight that change how wars are fought.
- **The Feast of Crows**: battlefields that feed scavengers, vermin and plague.

**Covers:** 3.2 RP3, 3.2 RP6, 3.2 RP8.

### 8. Wyrms and Hoards

*Not a species. A name, a lair, and a grudge.*

Meet the dragon of the northern peak by name. It remembers who wounded it, it counts its gold, and it wakes when you mine too close.

- **Named Beasts**: individual creatures with bodies, ages, lairs and memories.
- **Hoards Worth a Kingdom**: loot one and watch luxury prices crash.
- **Born of the Deep**: procedurally generated horrors with crystal scales, breath attacks and strange hungers.
- **Familiars**: beasts bound to mages, sharing their senses and their pain.
- **Monster Contracts**: bounties, professional hunters and the lore that makes them win.
- **The Ancient Ones Remember**: beasts that keep the true history of lost ages, and sell it.

**Covers:** 3.3a, 3.3a RP, 3.3a RP3 (familiars), 3.3a RP4 (contracts), 3.3a RP8, 3.3a RP9.

### 9. Plague Ledger

*It has a name. It has a host. And it learns.*

Diseases become living enemies. They mutate, ride caravans, hide in carriers and wait in frozen tombs. The cure is a race, and the winner can name the price.

- **Named Diseases**: pathogens with vectors, hosts and immunity that outlast every outbreak.
- **Evolving Plagues**: strains that grow deadlier, harder or resistant to treatment.
- **Silent Carriers**: the merchant who seemed healthy carried death across the sea.
- **Hard Choices**: close the ports, burn the dead or quarantine the poor quarter, and pay the price.
- **The Cure Race**: universities and monasteries compete to end the plague first.

**Covers:** 3.4, 3.4 RP, 3.4 RP3, F4.6.

## II. Body and Mind

### 10. Flesh and Bone

*A king can lose a hand. A kingdom can lose a king.*

Every wound has a place, a kind and a consequence. A severed sword hand ends a champion's career. A lost eye makes a seer in one culture and an outcast in another.

- **Bodies, Not Hit Points**: twenty-two body parts, tissue layers, arteries and nerves.
- **Wounds That Last**: scars, lost limbs and crippled knees carried for life.
- **Steel Meets Flesh**: material physics decides whether the blade cuts or the mail holds.
- **Healers and Surgeons**: herbs, sutures and amputations that turn death into survival.
- **The Silver Hand**: prostheses of wood, silver and star metal, some of them legendary.
- **Capacities**: what the body can still do sets what the person can still be.

**Covers:** F4.1–F4.5, F23, 3.1 (body attributes).

### 11. Curse of the Full Moon

*One bite. One desecrated grove. One night of the full moon.*

Curses, venoms, blessings and transformations share one deep system. They spread, they progress in stages, and some can only be lifted by a quest.

- **Effect Packages**: venoms, drugs, blessings and curses with onset, stages and cures.
- **Beasts Under the Moon**: shapeshifting curses passed by bite.
- **The Thirst**: undying hunters hiding behind false names.
- **Holy Ground Strikes Back**: desecrate a god's shrine and suffer that god's chosen curse.
- **Dependence**: addiction and withdrawal at the level of one life.

**Covers:** F30, 3.3a RP (night creatures), 8.6 RP (desecration curses).

### 12. The Inner Life

*They don't just act. They feel, they want and they remember.*

Every named person gets a mind built in layers: what they can do, how they react and what they believe. Grief, pride, envy and awe become forces in history.

- **Nineteen Attributes, Thirty-Six Facets, Twenty-Four Beliefs**: personalities with real depth.
- **Thoughts and Emotions**: eighty kinds of thought, twenty-four emotions, moods that rise and break.
- **Twenty-Two Needs**: from bread to beauty, faith to adventure.
- **Likes and Dislikes**: favourite foods, colours, creatures and art forms that shape markets and gifts.
- **Minds That Err**: overconfidence, sunk cost and paranoia make rulers fail in very human ways.
- **Ideas That Take Root**: beliefs incubate, then change a person for good.

**Covers:** F5 (personal), F8, F13, F14 (base), F15, F40, F13 RP5, F5 RP3.

### 13. Cradle to Pyre

*Every life is a story. Now every story has an arc.*

Follow mortals from infancy to old age. Childhood shapes them, youth tests them, careers lift them, and death comes in a hundred named ways.

- **Ages of a Life**: infancy, childhood, youth, adulthood, old age and, for some peoples, a long ancient age.
- **Pregnancy and Birth**: real risks and real complications.
- **Childhood Friends**: peer groups and play that become a king's inner council.
- **Careers with Ranks**: apprentice to grandmaster, soldier to marshal.
- **Life Dreams**: rule, create, avenge or find love, and despair when the dream dies.
- **Conviction**: those who live by their beliefs grow strong in crisis.

**Covers:** 3.1 (behaviours), 3.1 RP, 3.1 RP6, F6, F17, F17 RP3.

### 14. Hearts and Hands

*Friends, lovers, rivals and nemeses. All of them remember.*

Relationships become the fabric of the world. People befriend, flirt, insult and reconcile. The bitterest rivalries begin with one poorly chosen word.

- **Bonds with Two Axes**: friendship and romance, from crush to spouse, from rival to nemesis.
- **Twenty-One Interactions**: gossip, comfort, boast, court, quarrel, confide.
- **Burning Passions**: love, loyalty and hatred strong enough to win battles or break minds.
- **The Art of Persuasion**: appeals to belief, interest, authority, evidence or fear.
- **Pecking Orders**: who rules the warband, the court and the tavern table.
- **Duels of Wit**: riddles, insult verse and debates with stakes as high as a sword fight.

**Covers:** F7, F18, F18 RP3, F18 RP6.

### 15. Hearth and Home

*Behind every hero stands a household, and a supper table.*

Every mortal belongs to a household with a home, a purse and mouths to feed. What they eat shapes how tall they grow, and the tavern is where the world's news is told.

- **Households**: shared income, dependents, carers and the burden of the maimed.
- **Dwellings**: huts to palaces, each room shaping comfort and status.
- **You Are What You Eat**: famine generations grow shorter, sailors without fresh food fall sick.
- **Cuisines**: dishes of every culture, spreading with trade and taste.
- **Taverns and Inns**: hubs of gossip, recruitment and brawls.
- **Drink and Its Customs**: who may drink what, and on which holy night.

**Covers:** F29, F39, 4.2 RP5 (cuisines, taverns), 4.1 RP5 (intoxication, time budgets).

### 16. Strange Flesh

*Not every people breathes. Not every people dies.*

Twelve biologies beyond the human: spirits without bodies, peoples of the deep sea, hive minds, crystal folk, living plants and the undead. Each one lives, fights and worships in its own way.

- **Twelve Biologies**: incorporeal, aquatic, aerial, subterranean, hive, plant-like, construct, ageless, shapeshifting, gaseous, crystalline, undead.
- **Combine Them**: an aquatic hive or an ageless crystal people.
- **Their Own Wars**: three-dimensional sea battles, expendable hive soldiers, armies that fear nothing but holy fire.
- **Their Own Magic and Minds**: rites cast in scent and colour, constructs that never feel loss.

**Covers:** F46, F46 RP9.

## III. Society and Culture

### 17. Codes of Honour

*What is forbidden here is sacred there.*

Every culture keeps a ledger of right and wrong, and judges every act by it. Kill a guest, eat the forbidden meat or touch the impure, and the world will remember.

- **Ethics Tables**: forty acts, six stances, from required to unthinkable.
- **Honour, Face and Dignity**: three ways to carry shame and three ways to restore it.
- **Rules of the Room**: what is proper at a funeral, a feast or a court.
- **Purity and Pollution**: rites of cleansing, impure trades and hereditary castes.

**Covers:** F16, F16 RP4, F35, F41.

### 18. Ties of Blood

*The copper hair of the Varns runs true for nine generations.*

Families are genomes, lineages and alliances. Inheritance follows mothers or fathers, marriages bind clans, and the magical gift runs in the blood.

- **True Genetics**: looks, voice and heritable conditions passed by Mendelian rules.
- **Kinship Systems**: patrilineal, matrilineal, bilateral; cousins who are siblings and those who are not.
- **Marriage Alliances**: bride price, dowry, cross-cousin rules and clans that exchange brides.
- **Other Ways to Be Born**: peoples that change sex, choose it, bud or breed in castes.
- **The Gift in the Blood**: magical aptitude as an inherited trait, prized or hunted.

**Covers:** F26, F26 RP8, 4.1 RP4 (kinship, marriage, reproduction).

### 19. The Teeming Millions

*History is made by the many. Now you can count them.*

Populations become generations of real people by age, class and fate. Famines take the old and young, wars take the young men, and every generation remembers its youth.

- **Living Cohorts**: age, class and condition for every community.
- **Generations**: the war generation, the plague generation and the turnover that changes everything.
- **The Long Transition**: cities and schools lower birth rates and age the people.
- **Diasporas**: chain migration, remittances and foreign quarters.
- **Roles by Culture**: matriarchies, egalitarian clans and caste societies all from data.

**Covers:** F12, F12 RP3, 4.1 (demography, labour allocation), 4.1 RP3.

### 20. Many Banners

*One city. Divided by faith this decade, by class the next.*

Every person holds many identities: people, faith, class, guild, city. Which one matters most decides who fights whom, and ideas give each banner its creed.

- **Identity Salience**: invasions raise the nation, famines raise class, holy wars raise faith.
- **Ideologies**: schools of thought that spread through books and discontent.
- **Revolutions of the Mind**: egalitarian creeds that topple thrones, and creeds that defy the gods.
- **Revival of the Old Ways**: nativist and revitalisation movements under foreign rule.

**Covers:** F43, 8.5, 4.1 RP5 (revitalisation).

### 21. Fire in the Streets

*It started with one stone thrown at the bread line.*

Crowds have thresholds, and once crossed, the cascade cannot be stopped. Riots, panics and mass revivals emerge from the mood of a city.

- **Threshold Cascades**: small sparks that fade, or become a burning capital.
- **Mobs**: storm the granary, lynch the scapegoat, open the city gate.
- **Panic**: fire, plague rumour or an eclipse sends thousands running.
- **Fervour**: revivals, processions and mass conversions sweep through the streets.

**Covers:** F32.

### 22. Feast and Funeral

*Every wedding is a battlefield. Every funeral is a negotiation.*

Gatherings are where history happens: marriages are proposed, plots are hatched and guests are murdered. Traditions finally shape the lives of those who keep them.

- **Living Traditions**: festivals, fasts, taboos and rites with real costs and real effects.
- **Gatherings**: feasts, weddings, tournaments, hunts, councils and summits.
- **Marked Bodies**: tattoos, scars and jewellery that tell a life's deeds.
- **Names with Power**: name-giving rites, secret names and names of the dead not spoken.
- **Age-Sets**: warriors initiated together, rising together, loyal for life.

**Covers:** 8.3, 8.3 RP, F25, 4.1 RP5 (body modification, naming, age-sets).

### 23. The Gift

*A gift unreturned is a debt. A debt unpaid is a war.*

Exchange binds the world. Households, houses, kingdoms and even gods trade gifts, and every gift asks for one in return.

- **The Gift Ledger**: give, receive, return, or lose face.
- **Competitive Giving**: chiefs who destroy fortunes for rank.
- **Sacred Circuits**: treasures that travel forever between distant islands, binding them in peace.
- **Gifts to the Gods**: offerings that create divine debts, and gods who fail to repay them.

**Covers:** F38.

### 24. Muse and Market

*Art that moves a people. Forgeries that fool a king.*

Art has grammar, history and price. Cultures invent forms, artists found movements, collectors bid at auction, and the classics decide what a people believes.

- **Art Forms**: musical scales, poetic metres and dance styles that belong to a culture.
- **Art That Argues**: epics that make peoples weary of war, statues that keep resistance alive.
- **Troupes on the Road**: performers who carry songs, legends and secrets.
- **Art Markets and Forgery**: auctions, patrons and fakes that enter the canon.
- **Fashion Cycles**: what is new becomes common, and what is old returns.
- **Games of Skill and Chance**: board games that train generals, dice that ruin families.

**Covers:** 7.4, 7.4 RP, 7.4 RP4, 7.4 RP5, 7.5, 7.5 RP8, 4.3 RP4 (art movements).

### 25. Blues and Greens

*The race was lost. The tax was raised. The capital burned.*

Sport becomes politics. Clubs win fans, fans place bets, and when the fandoms follow faith and district lines, a lost race can end an empire.

- **Clubs and Leagues**: athletes, arenas, champions and seasons.
- **The Betting Floor**: fortunes made and lost on every match.
- **Circus Factions**: fans who become a political force.
- **Riots of the Hippodrome**: when sport and grievance meet.

**Covers:** 4.3 RP4 (sports, circus factions).

### 26. Tongues of Ages

*Every name means something. Every word has a history.*

Languages evolve by real sound laws, so cognates, dialects and lost tongues emerge from your world's history. Scholars decipher the dead and interpreters prevent wars.

- **Sound Change**: languages drift and split by contact, prestige and migration.
- **Grammar Matters**: word orders, cases and honorifics that make translation hard.
- **Scripts That Evolve**: pictograms to alphabets, borrowed and adapted.
- **Decipher the Dead**: bilingual stones and patient scholars unlock lost knowledge.
- **Barriers and Bridges**: misread treaties, lingua francas, pidgins, sign languages and thieves' cant.
- **Tongues of the Gods**: sacred languages whose words are rites.

**Covers:** 8.1, 8.1 RP, 8.1 RP3, 8.1 RP8, 8.1 RP9.

### 27. The Rewritten Past

*History is written by the victors. Until someone finds the old stone.*

Every event has a truth, and many tellings. Kings rewrite chronicles, peoples keep folk memories, and grievances from lost wars drive new ones centuries later.

- **Accounts and Truth**: official chronicles, folk memory and foreign records, measured against what really happened.
- **Damnatio Memoriae**: strike a name from history, deface every statue.
- **Revisionism**: new dynasties, new founding myths, old scandals waiting to be found.
- **Lost Lands**: irredentist claims born from old borders.
- **Days of Remembrance**: anniversaries that keep pride and grief alive.

**Covers:** F33.

## IV. Economy and City

### 28. Forge and Furnace

*No forests, no charcoal. No charcoal, no iron. No iron, no army.*

Production becomes a web of chains, methods and energy. A blockade on iron reaches the farmer through broken ploughs, and the first mills put weavers on the street.

- **Materials with Physics**: hardness, edge, sanctity and corruption for every material.
- **Sixteen Commodities**: grain to incense, timber to weapons.
- **Production Methods**: hand-loom or water-frame, bloomery or blast furnace.
- **Energy Geography**: rivers, winds, fuel, magic and divine power decide where industry rises.
- **Pollution**: smoke, slag and poisoned rivers with a price in health.
- **Dyes, Soaps and Powders**: chemistry from purple dye to explosives.

**Covers:** F3, F3 RP3, F24, 6.3, 6.3 RP, 6.3 RP5.

### 29. Coin of the Realm

*Debase the coin, and the merchants will never forget.*

Markets, money and credit come alive. Prices swing with harvests, bankers lend to warlords, and speculators hoard grain while cities starve.

- **Living Markets**: prices that respond to supply, demand and distance.
- **Currencies**: mint, debase, inflate and lose the merchants' trust.
- **Banks and Credit**: interest rates, defaults and wars over unpaid debts.
- **Boom and Bust**: speculation and hoarding that create cycles nobody planned.
- **Insurance**: pooled risk and the merchants who gamble because of it.
- **Exchange and Saving**: money changers, bills of exchange and buried hoards.

**Covers:** 6.4 (markets, money), 6.4 RP, 6.4 RP5 (insurance), 6.4 RP6 (saving, exchange rates).

### 30. The Salt Road

*Goods, gods and plagues travel the same roads.*

Trade routes become living arteries. Caravans carry silk and news, ships carry spices and disease, and whoever holds the pass holds the purse.

- **Named Trade Routes**: roads, rivers and sea lanes with capacity and tolls.
- **Caravans and Convoys**: cargo, guards, passengers and the bandits who wait for them.
- **The Right Vessel**: perishables by speed, livestock on the hoof, bulk by water.
- **Ports and Warehouses**: throughput, speculation and the art of cornering a market.
- **Trading Posts Abroad**: merchant houses with outposts in foreign cities.
- **Travellers for Pleasure**: tourism to wonders, baths and battlefields.

**Covers:** 6.4 (trade routes), 6.4 RP3, 6.4 RP6 (containers, tourism, vertical integration), 4.3 RP3 (trading posts).

### 31. Yoke and Wage

*Who works the fields? Who owns them? Who is free?*

Labour has a price, and sometimes a chain. Wages set standards of living, mills create the unemployed, and conquest feeds the slave markets.

- **Jobs and Wages**: workplaces, earnings and the cost of living by class.
- **Tiered Needs**: meet them and rise in class, fail them and fall.
- **The Unemployed**: crime, emigration and recruits for every rebellion.
- **Corvée and Peonage**: labour obligations and debts that never end.
- **Slave Markets and Freedom**: where it is lawful, captives are sold, and some buy their freedom.

**Covers:** 4.1 RP (jobs, wages, need tiers, unemployment), 4.9, 4.9 RP4.

### 32. Walls and Wards

*Every city grows its own way. Every city burns its own way.*

Settlements become cities of quarters, services and slums. Great fires reshape the streets, the rich push out the poor, and every rebuild is a chance to do better or worse.

- **Districts**: markets, harbours, temple quarters, slums and necropolises.
- **Desirability**: amenities and nuisances that set rents and who lives where.
- **Services by Reach**: wells, watch posts and healers covering the streets around them.
- **Houses That Grow**: huts become townhouses when needs are met.
- **Fire, Filth and Disease**: wooden quarters burn, crowded ones sicken.
- **Charity**: almshouses and orphanages that keep children out of gangs.
- **Ready for the Worst**: levees, granaries and firebreaks, then aid and rebuilding after disaster.

**Covers:** 4.2, 4.2 RP, 4.2 RP3 (except turf), 4.2 RP4 (preparedness, relief, reconstruction), 4.2 RP5 (welfare, commuting).

### 33. Wonders Rising

*Generations of labour. One wonder. One rival who finished first.*

Structures take time, staff and upkeep. Wonders span reigns, rivals race to finish first, and megastructures reach out to the stars themselves.

- **Forty Structure Kinds**: each with a real function, from granary to observatory.
- **Building Takes Time**: wonders that outlive the kings who began them.
- **Building Materials**: mud brick to hydraulic concrete, each with its own strength and weakness.
- **The Wonder Race**: one world, one wonder, many builders.
- **Megastructures**: star cages, orbital rings and planet engines.
- **Museums and Gardens**: collections that draw scholars, and sacred groves that draw gods.
- **Lifting Water**: waterwheels and pumps that open terraces and deep mines.

**Covers:** 7.2, 7.2 RP, 7.2 RP6, 7.2 RP7, F3 RP6.

### 34. Heirlooms and Relics

*The sword that killed three kings. The crown that made the fourth.*

Items carry history. Swords gather renown with every kill, crowns grant legitimacy, and saints' bones heal by chance and draw pilgrims across the world.

- **Provenance**: every maker, owner and deed recorded.
- **Decorations That Remember**: shields engraved with the victory at Varn.
- **Artifacts**: masterworks born of obsession, coveted by all.
- **Regalia**: without the crown, the king is just a man.
- **Relics**: bones of saints and fragments of dead gods.
- **Lenses and Clocks**: spectacles that extend careers, clocks that order the day.
- **Locks and Archives**: vaults against thieves, papyrus that rots and clay that survives.

**Covers:** 7.1, 7.1 RP, 7.1 RP5, 7.1 RP6.

### 35. Underworld

*Every city has two masters. Only one of them has a crown.*

Crime organises. Families run rackets, thieves' guilds keep their codes, and turf wars bleed the streets while the watch takes its cut.

- **Criminal Organisations**: crime families, smuggling rings and pirate brotherhoods.
- **Rackets**: protection, gambling, fencing and counterfeiting.
- **Turf Wars**: districts claimed and defended with blood.
- **Officials on the Payroll**: bribes, hooks and silence.
- **Thief-Takers and Bounty Hunters**: those who hunt the outlaws for coin.

**Covers:** 4.3 RP3 (crime organisations), 4.2 RP3 (turf), 4.5 RP3 (thief-takers, bounty hunters).

## V. Power and Politics

### 36. Throne and Council

*Wear the crown. Fear the council. Name your heir.*

Realms become true states with governments, offices, treasuries and succession laws. Legitimacy is earned and lost, and empires that grow too fast tear themselves apart.

- **Ten Kinds of Government**: chiefdoms to god-kingdoms.
- **Offices of State**: chancellors, marshals and spymasters with tasks of their own.
- **Legitimacy**: law, victory, faith and an unbroken body.
- **Succession Crises**: regencies, usurpers and wars of the heirs.
- **Overextension**: provinces that drift when the realm outgrows its scribes.
- **Taxes, Mandates and Corruption**: how the realm takes, commands and leaks.
- **Tiers at Every Level**: hamlet to metropolis, tribe to hegemon.

**Covers:** 4.6, 4.6 RP (council tasks, corruption, taxes, mandates, demands, constitutions, court, separatism, policy networks).

### 37. Patrons and Puppets

*The throne is held by whoever the throne must keep happy.*

*An eternal storm. A road of ghosts. Two histories, both true.*

Persistent phenomena break the rules of their regions: crystal blooms, time slips and god-corpse auras that last for centuries.

- **Phenomena**: eternal storms, living fogs, dead zones and singing stones.
- **They Grow**: phenomena spread unless warded.
- **Rich and Dangerous**: harvest reagents at crystal blooms, open gates at leak wounds.
- **Contested Histories**: two incompatible pasts, until one becomes legend.
- **Beyond the Land**: phenomena in the sky, across a whole star system and along routes.

**Covers:** 10.6, 10.6 RP3, 10.6 RP10.

### 78. Between the Stars

*Wait for the window. Then sail into the void.*

Travel between worlds becomes a voyage of windows, fuel and danger, and along the busiest routes, a culture of the stars is born.

- **Launch Windows**: the dance of orbits decides when ships can sail.
- **Fuel and Range**: run short and the crew starves.
- **Cargo and Convoys**: colonists, relics and stowaway plagues.
- **Encounters**: pirates, storms, void beasts and derelicts.
- **Survey Levels**: rumoured, observed, visited, mapped.
- **Cosmopolitan Cultures**: shared tongues and stories across worlds.

**Covers:** 10.4, 10.4 RP5, 10.5, 10.2 RP (survey levels).

### 79. Deep Time

*Before you arrived, ten thousand years had already passed.*

New worlds can begin with a simulated prehistory: language families, ruined capitals, extinct beasts, dormant crowns and ancient grudges.

- **Simulated Prehistory**: centuries of history before play begins.
- **Ruins and Relics**: cities that rose and fell before your first epoch.
- **Old Grudges**: wars remembered in opposite ways.
- **Fast-Forward**: run a world ahead and read what happened.
- **Every World Its Own Past**: dreamt pasts, voyage histories and the arrival of souls.

**Covers:** F48, F48 RP9, F48 RP10.

### 80. The Turning Wheel

*This age will end. Another will begin. You will remember.*

A cosmos that turns in cycles. Every apocalypse becomes a new beginning, and the roles of the last age return in new faces.

- **Cyclic Ages**: the end is a turning, not a close.
- **Echoes**: the betrayer, the reborn hero and the founding couple, again.
- **Fragments of the Past**: half-remembered texts from the last age.
- **Souls Returning**: heroes of the past reborn in their echoes.

**Covers:** 10.8 RP4.

## XI. Knowledge and Eras

### 81. Sparks of Genius

*One discovery can change everything. One great mind can make it.*

Knowledge becomes a living tree that each people climbs at its own pace. Need drives invention, institutions spread across the land, and great people change history.

- **Holder Levels**: every people knows what it knows.
- **The Civic Tree**: law, bureaucracy, banking, universities and philosophy.
- **Spreading Institutions**: ideas that rise in one city and sweep a continent.
- **Eureka**: survive a siege and learn fortification.
- **Great People**: scholars, generals and prophets who change everything.
- **Golden and Dark Ages**: eras of glory, collapse and heroic recovery.
- **Dangerous Knowledge**: experiments that end in fire, plague or worse.

**Covers:** 8.4, 8.4 RP.

### 82. The Scholar's Lamp

*The physicians bled the patient. The patient died. Who was wrong?*

Theories can be wrong, and wrong theories kill. Scholars fight over paradigms, schools of thought keep heresies alive, and examinations turn scholars into rulers.

- **True and False Theories**: humours, geocentrism and contagion, each with real consequences.
- **Paradigm Shifts**: anomalies accumulate until the old truth falls.
- **Schools of Thought**: masters, disciples and rival lineages.
- **Examinations**: officials chosen by merit, and the elites they create.
- **Academic Freedom**: free minds discover, censored ones flee.
- **Printing and Censorship**: cheap books, fast ideas, banned texts.
- **Numbers**: bases, zero and the precision they bring.

**Covers:** 8.4b, 8.4 RP4, 8.4 RP5, 8.4 RP (printing, censorship).

### 83. Steam and Lightning

*No ceiling. No end. Only what your history can reach.*

Worlds can advance beyond the ancient and the magical, into gunpowder, steam, electricity, information and spaceflight.

- **Seven Eras**: classical to advanced, with no cap.
- **The Industrial Turn**: mills, railways, factories and the new working class.
- **Wars of a New Age**: muskets, artillery, rifles and aircraft.
- **Instant Messages**: telegraph and radio collapse the delays of the world.
- **Rockets**: non-magical flight between worlds.
- **Magic and Machine**: iron that disrupts rites, theories that unite them.
- **Guarded by Plausibility**: no single genius ends the cosmos.

**Covers:** F44.

### 84. Fateweaver

*The world writes its own stories. Fateweaver makes them sing.*

A deterministic storyteller and drama manager that paces disasters and golden ages, opens storylets at dramatic moments and keeps characters' arcs on course.

- **The Storyteller**: steady, rising, cyclic or chaotic pacing.
- **Storylets**: the stranger at the gate, the dying king and his two sons.
- **Character Arcs**: revenge, redemption, rise and fall.
- **Counterpoint**: a rival's rise timed against the hero's fall.
- **Setups and Payoffs**: prophecies and omens that come back when they matter.

**Covers:** F27, F34, F36.

### 85. Into the Dark

*Pack the torches. Count the rations. Not everyone comes back.*

Expeditions into ruins, wildernesses and underworlds. Parties carry light and food, gain quirks and afflictions, and dig up the past room by room.

- **Expeditions**: scouts, guides, healers and porters.
- **Light and Hunger**: darkness raises danger, rations run out.
- **Afflictions and Virtues**: some break, some rise.
- **Quirks**: "afraid of the deep", "knows the old tunnels".
- **Sites with Rooms**: towers, vaults, lairs and tombs explored step by step.
- **Excavations**: five stages of digging, finds and curses.

**Covers:** 6.2 RP, 6.2 RP4.
